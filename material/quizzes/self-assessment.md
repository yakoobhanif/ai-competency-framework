Version 2 replaces performance predictions with self-ratings of current capability. It retains the four domains and 19 capability IDs and supersedes version 1's percentage estimates and calibration-gap calculations.

Version 2.1 aligns each question more explicitly with the criteria in Section E and their evidence routes in Section F. The shared five-point scale and 19-item structure are unchanged. Brief scope notes clarify the broad conceptual items without providing quiz answers.

Version 2.2 aligns administration and evidence routes with the simplified platform. L4 now concerns explanation and application while using AI; the upload-only task cannot verify unaided understanding or retention. The other 18 questions and response scale are unchanged.

Version 2.3 retains the 19 capabilities and five response labels, but replaces generic descriptions with capability-specific, behaviourally anchored self-ratings. These are proposed descriptors for pilot testing, not validated proficiency levels. Higher ratings indicate greater depth, accuracy and completeness within the same capability, not specialist technical knowledge or more AI use.

Version 2.4 implements the agreed 25-capability framework: U5-U9 and C6 are added, understanding covers AI generally, and U7 concerns model architecture. New descriptors are draft pilot anchors. Technical detail is expected where the capability calls for it; no specialist knowledge is inferred from fluent AI use.

Version 2.5 removes question subtitles and the redundant Skip action. Each self-assessment question has one complete prompt, five ability ratings and Not sure. The wording and controls are identical in demo and live mode.

Version 2.6 implements the approved wording review across all 25 questions and their answer descriptions. Domain transitions are explicit, middle ratings distinguish developing or inconsistent performance where appropriate, and U6 combines keeping up with AI developments with understanding their implications. The response labels, Not sure option and scoring are unchanged.

## Part A. Student Questionnaire

### Instructions

This questionnaire asks about your **current ability to understand and use AI in your university studies**. AI includes rule-based, predictive and generative systems, such as recommendation, perception and language tools. Learning and creation may use any suitable AI approach, including agents.

Think about what you can do now, based on your knowledge and experience. For learning and creation, consider coursework at your current level. You do not need programming experience: creating work can mean producing a report or presentation, not only writing code.

Rate your own ability to use and judge AI, rather than what an AI tool can do on its own. For questions about understanding a topic, consider what you can explain yourself.

There are 25 questions. Allow about seven minutes; timing remains provisional. There are no right or wrong self-assessment answers, and your responses do not affect your quiz or practical-task marks. Please answer without using AI or asking someone else for help.

### Response Scale

Use the same five labels for every question. Each question gives its own descriptions of what those levels mean:

| Response | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1 - Not at all | See the description for this question. |
| 2 - Slightly | See the description for this question. |
| 3 - Moderately | See the description for this question. |
| 4 - Well | See the description for this question. |
| 5 - Very well | See the description for this question. |
| Not sure | I do not have enough knowledge or experience to judge my ability. |

Choose the description that best matches what you can currently do, considering the whole ability rather than only its easiest part. Use **Not sure**, rather than the middle response, if you cannot judge your ability. Strong performance requires clear explanations appropriate to the capability, not longer conversations or unnecessary revisions.

## Part B. Administration and Interpretation

### 1. Source Review and Design Rationale

These references are already in the working document. They inform the language and structure, not a claim that this exact 25-item instrument has been validated.

| Reference and material reviewed | Relevant design feature | Application here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carolus et al. (2023), [MAILS development paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.09319), questionnaire-development section | Specific, present-tense ability statements rated on a common 0-10 ability scale. | Ask about current ability using a consistent format, not predicted marks. |
| Koch et al. (2024), [MAILS short questionnaire](https://downloads.hci.informatik.uni-wuerzburg.de/MAILS-short-questionnaire-english.pdf) and [validation paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e39686) | Compact ability statements, shared instructions and a 0-10 response scale. | Retain short ability questions and common labels; our item-specific descriptors are a new adaptation, not original MAILS wording. |
| Wang, Rau and Yuan (2022 online; 2023 issue), [AI Literacy Scale](https://doi.org/10.1080/0144929X.2022.2072768), full paper, administration section and Table 5 | Twelve self-report items spanning awareness, usage, evaluation and ethics, with seven response levels. | Use ordinary language about current knowledge, ability and responsible use. |
| Hershkovitz et al. (2025), [task-centered GenAI literacy study](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-8954/13/7/518), Table 2 and questionnaire methods | Eight present-tense knowledge/ability statements anchored to a study task, rated on a five-point agreement scale. | Ground the questions in coursework. Our version uses direct ability questions rather than agreement statements or a single chosen task. |
| Gumus and Kara (2025), [GenAI Literacy for Learning Scale](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/view/10236), publisher abstract and reported construct structure | Covers learning needs, prompt/language skills, autonomous learning and critical thinking. | Retain learning needs, regulation and student reasoning. The final standalone questionnaire wording was not verified and is not copied here. |

**Our adaptation:** All items use "How well can you...?" with a five-point ability scale. This is a project-specific adaptation, not the original MAILS or Wang scale, nor Hershkovitz's agreement scale. Wording is original and follows our framework. Published reliability and validity findings do not transfer to these new items or this response scale.

Version 2.3 adds original capability-specific response descriptions. Levels progress within each construct rather than escalating into unrelated technical expertise. The descriptions avoid worked answers to the subsequent knowledge quiz, activity counts, unnecessary revisions and promises of future behaviour. They are self-report anchors, not objective rubric scores. Check ordering, comprehension, ceiling effects and the extra reading time in the pilot; five minutes remains a provisional estimate. Specific descriptions may influence how students interpret later tasks, so administer the same frozen form to comparison groups and review possible priming during validation.

GLAT informs the separate objective quiz, not self-report wording. Anthropic informs practical indicators, not a validated self-assessment instrument. Kang and Park's higher-education scale remains conceptual context from the working document; its full item wording was not verified for this revision.

The scale measures perceived capability, not usage frequency, future intention or demonstrated proficiency. One item per capability keeps the questionnaire short but cannot represent every subcriterion or establish capability-level reliability by itself.

### 2. Administration

1. Collect the name and applicable consent on the overview screen. Store the name separately from the opaque attempt ID and exclude it from grading inputs. Do not add demographic questions to these 25 items.
2. Present Part A before the quiz, practical tasks or feedback. Ask students about current university work, not predicted performance on an unseen assessment.
3. Use identical wording and scales for both creation pathways. Coding is not a general prerequisite. Record the practical pathway separately; do not assign it based on self-ratings.
4. Target five minutes without a hard cutoff or speed penalty. Check timing and comprehension in student dry runs and provide approved accessibility support.
5. Present one question per screen with Back/Next and saved progress. Show five labelled ability levels and `Not sure`, with no Skip action and no default. Keep the shared scale available. On paper, students circle one response.
6. Students respond without AI or another person's advice. Administrators may explain navigation or the response scale, but must not recommend answers.
7. Allow omissions and distinguish them from `Not sure`. Invite review without forcing an invented rating. Lock responses on submission; a later self-assessment is a separate time point.
8. Hide self-ratings from task assistants, automated graders and human raters until independent scoring is finalized. They must not affect task difficulty, allowed time or marks.

### 3. Scoring

Each capability receives the selected **1-5 rating**. Higher ratings indicate greater self-reported ability. There is no answer key, reverse coding or automatic proficiency classification.

`Not sure` and unanswered items have no numeric value. Do not substitute zero, the midpoint or an average. A rating of 1 is valid, not missing.

Report all 25 ratings and labels. Optional domain summaries are descriptive, equal-weight means on the original scale:

```text
Understand AI = (U1 + U2 + U3 + U4 + U5 + U6 + U7 + U8 + U9) / 9
Learn and Solve with AI = (L1 + L2 + L3 + L4) / 4
Create with AI = (C1 + C2 + C3 + C4 + C5 + C6) / 6
Evaluate and Use AI Responsibly = (R1 + R2 + R3 + R4 + R5 + R6) / 6
```

Calculate a domain mean only when at least 80% of its items have numeric responses, averaging those responses. Otherwise leave it blank and keep the numeric-response count, such as `3 of 4 rated`. Display means to one decimal place and retain full precision internally. These ordinal-rating means are convenience summaries, not validated interval measurements or evidence of a single underlying factor. Keep L4 and C5 individually visible even when domain means are shown.

Do not calculate an overall competency total, pass/fail result, percentile or population rank. A rating of 4 does not mean 80% competence or map automatically to a practical-rubric level.

| R4 | Relevant information/security exposure and effective protection or safe alternative | One quiz question; NOUL evidence from the task transcripts when demonstrated |
| R5 | Compliance with assignment, attribution and licensing rules; accurate and complete disclosure | One quiz question; NOUL evidence from the task transcripts when demonstrated |
| R6 | Appropriate final decision and a justification proportionate to evidence, consequences and remaining limits | One quiz question; NOUL evidence from the task transcripts when demonstrated |

Boundary checks: U4 asks about understanding information availability, while C3 asks about actually providing useful inputs. L3 concerns the student's own reasoning; R2 concerns appraisal of AI reasoning. R1 concerns evidence checks; C4 concerns implementing decisions; C5 concerns the finished result. R6 concerns the final decision, not repeating the checks that informed it. These distinctions should be preserved in both item revisions and scorer rubrics.

The questions now represent the main scoring criteria more explicitly, but a single rating still summarizes a broader capability. It does not supply separate self-ratings for each rubric criterion or reproduce every task-specific anchor. In particular, R3/R4 quiz scores demonstrate scenario judgement, not proof of responsible conduct in every real situation. The practical follow-up remains necessary.

Retain self-ratings on their original 1-5 scale in research records. The student results page shows dimension categories, not numeric capability scores. For the optional radar overlay only, project the equal-weight dimension mean using `(mean - 1) * 25`; require at least 80% of the dimension's self-ratings. This is a visual comparison, not an equivalent performance measurement. Practical evidence can only add to the quiz baseline, under the rules in `portal/docs/scoring-contract.md`. Absent or unresolved practical evidence preserves the quiz baseline, with unresolved evidence flagged. General and technical creation are alternative pathways, not two scores to average. C5's pass/fail result is a much coarser observation than its self-rating.

**Do not subtract these scales.** The old percentage-point calibration-gap calculation no longer applies. Rescaling a self-rating to 0-100 does not make it equivalent to a quiz percentage or practical score. Do not automatically classify students as overconfident or underconfident.

Suggested report wording:

> You rated your ability to verify AI-generated claims as "Well" (4 of 5). In this task, you compared an explanation with a slide but did not explain whether they agreed. A useful next step is to interpret what your checks establish. Your self-rating concerns broader experience; this result describes the submitted task.

Research can examine relationships across participants using methods appropriate to ordinal data, such as rank correlations. These are not individual calibration scores. Any future numerical discrepancy measure needs a justified, validated linking method. One task cannot establish general capability or the accuracy of a student's self-knowledge.

### 5. Minimum Data Contract

Store one response per capability, retaining all 25 IDs even when unanswered. Keep submitted responses immutable.

```text
Attempt metadata:
  instrument_version: self_assessment_v2.6
  framework_reference: working-doc-D-E-F
  attempt_id: opaque ID
  participant_key: pseudonymous ID
  timepoint: baseline
  started_at: server timestamp
  submitted_at: server timestamp or null while draft
  status: draft | submitted
```

Example of an answered item, not a preselected value:

```json
{
  "capability_id": "L2",
  "response_type": "current_ability_1_5",
  "response_status": "answered",
  "value": 4
}
```

- `answered`: integer value 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5.
- `not_sure`: `value: null`.
- `missing`: `value: null`.

Reject unknown or duplicate IDs, numeric strings, booleans, fractional ratings and out-of-range values. Validate status against value. Compute descriptive means in code, not with an LLM.

Preserve version 1 records under their original version. Do not convert their percentages or 0-3 predictions into version 2 ratings, pool the versions as one scale, or apply the old gap formula to version 2. Link performance evidence by attempt ID, retaining its task, quiz, rubric and pathway versions separately. Apply approved study access and retention policies.

Keep versions 2.0, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.4 and 2.5 separately identifiable. L4's scope changed in 2.2; response descriptions changed in 2.3. Do not assume equivalence, relabel old responses or silently replace an instrument during a cohort.

### 6. Verification and Pilot Checks

| Check | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Coverage | Exactly U1-U9, L1-L4, C1-C6 and R1-R6, with no duplicates |
| Consistency | Every item has the same 1-5 scale plus Not sure |
| L ratings 2, 3, 4, 5 | Mean 3.5; coverage 4 of 4 |
| L ratings 2, 3, 4, Not sure | No mean (75% is below 80%); coverage 3 of 4; retain not_sure |
| L ratings 2, 3, 4, blank | No mean; coverage 3 of 4; retain missing |
| Four L ratings of 1 | Mean 1.0; coverage 4 of 4 |
| Rating 0 or 6 | Reject as invalid, not as missing |
| Self-rating 4; observed score 60% | Show both on their own scales; no arithmetic gap |

Before cohort use, check that first-year students understand the wording and distinguish uncertainty from moderate ability. Review whether broad capability items, especially L4 and R3-R5, need multiple items in a future research version. Check ceiling effects, socially desirable responses, subgroup interpretation and test-retest stability. Investigate the proposed domain structure rather than inheriting source-scale validity. Record completion times and revise the five-minute target if necessary. Freeze wording within a cohort; changes require a new instrument version.
