# Essay Task: Do Recommendation Algorithms Shape Our Preferences?

Suggested time: 20 minutes. You may use up to 40 additional minutes from the practical-task allowance.

## Your assignment

You're taking a university class on technology and society. For your next assignment, write an essay of **at most 1,000 words** answering:

**Do recommendation algorithms shape our preferences, or do they mainly learn preferences we already have?**

Write for a university reader who is unfamiliar with recommendation algorithms. You may use examples such as music, shopping, video or news recommendations. Take a clear position and explain your reasoning. Your argument can draw on both views.

Explain how recommendation algorithms can learn existing preferences and shape new ones. Consider a strong counterargument or alternative explanation, and use at least one concrete example.

Support your main claims with **at least two identifiable sources** that you have checked. These may include research papers, publications from established institutions or technical explanations with clear authorship. Be clear about what your sources show and what you conclude from them, and acknowledge an important limitation in your argument. You'll need to find your own sources.

The word limit includes the title, headings and in-text citations, but excludes the bibliography. There is no minimum word count. Focus on a clear, well-supported argument rather than length.

## What to do

Write the essay as you normally would. You are encouraged to use an LLM to help you plan, research, draft or review it, in whatever order works for you. You may also work without AI. We want to understand your usual approach to creating work.

Cite borrowed ideas and quotations directly in the essay.

## Deliverable rubric

Your essay is assessed against the five requirements below. You can argue for either view or a combination of both.

| Criterion | Required standard |
| --- | --- |
| Argument | Answers the question directly and supports a clear, reasoned position. |
| Analysis | Explains how algorithms learn and shape preferences, and considers a strong counterargument or alternative explanation. |
| Evidence | Uses a concrete example and at least two identifiable sources supporting substantive claims. |
| Judgement | Acknowledges an important limitation and draws a conclusion supported by the evidence. |
| Communication and format | Clear, organized writing within the 1,000-word limit, with a bibliography. |
